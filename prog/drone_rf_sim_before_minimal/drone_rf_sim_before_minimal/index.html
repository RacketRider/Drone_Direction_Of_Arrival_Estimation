Model notes

This page simulates RF direction finding of a drone in a 3D outdoor scene. Each receiver estimates a bearing only. Two or more separated receivers combine their bearings to estimate a horizontal position.

1. Scene geometry and materials

Every tree trunk, foliage canopy and rock is a closed triangle mesh. The ray tracer uses the same triangles that you see. Each object has its own relative permittivity εr, effective conductivity σ and permeability μr. From these the page computes complex permittivity εc = εr − jσ/(ωε0), refractive index n = √(εc μr), attenuation α = k0·|Im n| (shown in dB/m), skin depth, wavelength inside the material, intrinsic impedance and Brewster angle. The values are representative presets near 2.4 GHz, not measurements of a specific site.

2. Propagation (deterministic ray optics)

  • Direct path: exact segment–triangle intersection. Each crossed object adds Fresnel interface loss at the actual facet angles (entry and exit), bulk attenuation e^{−αL} and extra phase (β−k0)L. It uses a straight-line (slab) approximation: refraction bending of this path is not followed. The visual probe does follow it.
  • Ground reflection: image method on y = 0 with full TE/TM Fresnel coefficients for the chosen soil (water inside puddles), times the Rayleigh roughness factor exp(−8(πσh cosθi/λ)²).
  • Facet reflections: exact image method on every flat facet of rocks and trunks, with a physical-optics correction when a facet is smaller than the first Fresnel zone. Second-order ground→facet and facet→ground paths are included.
  • Diffraction: ITU-R P.526 single knife-edge loss over the top and around both sides of each hard obstacle that blocks the direct path.
  • Diffuse scattering: Lambertian effective-roughness model (Degli-Esposti) for rocks and trunks, and isotropic volume scattering for canopies. These paths are added incoherently (as power).
  • Polarisation is carried as a complex 3-vector and split into TE/TM components at every interaction. The drone and receivers use dipole-like patterns.

3. Receiver and DoA

Each receiver is an N-beam switched directional array with one RF chain, the same model as the project's doa_sim.py. For every beam the coherent drone paths are summed with that beam's gain. Interferers and noise are added, and the measured power is drawn as an average of L samples (Gamma distribution). PV-ML fits the power vector to the known beam patterns by maximum likelihood. Bartlett and MUSIC are shown for comparison on an equivalent coherent circular array, which needs N phase-synchronised receive chains. A Kalman filter tracks each bearing. Bearings are fused by weighted least squares, with an error ellipse from the covariance.

4. Wave view (2D FDTD)

When a trunk, canopy or rock is selected, a 2D TMz Yee-grid FDTD solves Maxwell's equations on a horizontal cross-section through the real mesh, at the chosen frequency. Neighbouring objects are included. A plane wave arrives from the drone's azimuth, and a graded absorbing layer surrounds the domain. It shows reflection, refraction (shorter wavelength inside), absorption, shadowing and diffraction directly. It is a 2D approximation (objects treated as infinitely long vertically) of a local patch, not a 3D full-scene Maxwell solution.

5. Limitations to state in the report

  • Material values are illustrative presets. Real values depend on frequency, moisture, temperature and mineral content.
  • The ray model is high-frequency asymptotic, not full-wave. Facet–facet multi-bounce paths and transmission-plus-reflection combinations are not traced.
  • The RF data are synthetic. No SDR hardware is connected, and nothing here is a field-validated detection claim.
  • The arrays are azimuth-only, so drone altitude is not estimated by the localiser.
